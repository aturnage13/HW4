# Campus Customs Design *(Problem 10)*

Most of the site's creative design came from the usability work in Problem 9. Here's what changed and why it keeps shoppers around long enough to buy.

| What we changed | Why it helps customers stick around and buy |
|---|---|
| **Yale logo in a Yale Blue top bar** | Instant recognition: shoppers know right away this is Yale gear, which builds trust from the first second. |
| **Campus photos** (Harkness Tower banner, Old Campus, Sterling, Phelps Gate, Cross Campus) | Reminds people how pretty campus is *while they're deciding to spend money*. Nostalgia and pride make the gear feel worth having. |
| **Grey pages under the blue bar** | More contrast: navigation stands apart from content, the page feels calm and uncluttered, and product photos pop. |
| **Serif headings, softer cards, hover zoom, photo category tiles** | Feels like a polished collegiate shop. Hover effects signal "click me" and invite browsing. |
| **Handsome Dan for "we don't have it" and "found it"** | Turns a dead end into a smile, so people keep looking instead of leaving, and gives the shop a lovable, memorable mascot. |
| **Rotating Yale-specific one-liners** ("busy beating Harvard…", "Lux et Veritas, and also these!") | Small surprises reward browsing and make the site fun to come back to. |
| **Perky, engaging chat with tap-to-ask suggestions** | Feels like a friendly in-store clerk, and the follow-up questions and one-tap suggestions keep people chatting and browsing closer to checkout. |
| **Clean Harvard chirps** (never rude) | Playful rivalry is part of the Yale experience. It's shareable and keeps the brand friendly for every shopper. |
| **Chat searches fill the page with product cards** | Customers see everything that matches at once, with pictures and prices, so it takes fewer steps to find the thing they'll buy. |
